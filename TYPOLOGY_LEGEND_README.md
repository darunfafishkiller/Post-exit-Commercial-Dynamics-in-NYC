# Commercial System Typology Legend

This document describes the **Area**, **Corridor**, and **Small Node** types shown on the interactive map.

---

## Area Types

### A1 — Dense High-FAR Mixed Cores

A1 Areas are the densest, most intensively developed commercial cores in New York City’s commercial system. They typically combine high floor-area ratios (FAR), strong transit accessibility, and a high degree of land-use mixing, with commerce closely layered with office, residential, food, and service uses. Relative to other Area types, they most closely resemble mature urban commercial centers: large in commercial scale, functionally composite, and sustained in activity. Although only six Areas fall into this type, their high-density, high-accessibility, and mixed-use character is highly consistent, so A1 reads as a clear set of higher-order commercial centers rather than a residual statistical class.

### A2 — Chain-Oriented Dispersed Retail Fields

A2 Areas are distinguished by a high share of chain retail and a relatively dispersed spatial organization. Establishments do not necessarily form a continuous, compact street frontage; they may instead occupy larger lots, parking fields, shopping centers, roadside strips, or separated commercial clusters. Their commercial role tends toward standardized retail and destination-oriented consumption, and visitors may rely on cars, transit, or longer trips rather than a continuous walkable shopping environment. They can be understood as sizable but weakly continuous commercial fields dominated by chain retail.

### A3 — Uniform High-Density Mixed Fields

A3 Areas combine relatively high and evenly distributed commercial density with mixed commercial functions. Unlike A1’s higher-order urban cores, A3 is not defined primarily by extreme FAR or strong centrality. Instead, commercial activity is continuous and balanced across the Area, without strong concentration on a few isolated nodes. Retail, food, services, and other everyday commercial uses typically coexist, forming a stable high-density commercial environment. These Areas can be read as mature, continuous urban commercial districts with relatively limited internal contrast.

### A4 — Compact Low-FAR Service Clusters

A4 Areas are usually compact in extent, but overall built intensity and FAR remain comparatively low. Their commercial mix leans toward local services and everyday needs—personal services, neighborhood retail, food, repair, and other resident-oriented activities. Establishments may cluster on a small block, at an intersection, or in a localized commercial pocket, without forming a high-rise, high-intensity urban core. They function as community-oriented commercial clusters embedded in residential or low-rise urban settings, oriented more to neighborhood service than to regional draw.

### A5 — Extensive Food-Oriented Connected Fields

A5 Areas typically cover a larger spatial extent, with strong internal continuity and connectivity and a high share of food- and beverage-related commerce. Activity often unfolds along multiple linked streets or commercial corridors rather than around a single center. They combine scale with walkable continuity and internal network connection, and they are distinctly characterized by restaurants, cafés, bars, or food retail. These Areas can be understood as spatially extensive commercial districts with strong street connectivity and a pronounced food-oriented profile.

### A6 — Diversified Connected Mixed Fields

A6 Areas combine strong internal connectivity with a high diversity of commercial functions. Different forms of retail, food, daily services, and other commercial activities coexist along connected streets or commercial spaces, usually without a single use dominating. Relative to A5, they are not identified primarily by food orientation; the emphasis is instead on functional diversity and a more comprehensive commercial network. They can be understood as structurally coherent, relatively balanced, and internally well-connected mixed commercial environments.

## Corridor Types

### C0 — Peaked Compact Intensity Corridors

C0 Corridors are compact, high-intensity commercial corridors that typically stand as relatively independent linear cores. Commercial activity concentrates over a short distance and shows a clear local intensity peak. They are usually not the everyday organizational backbone inside a larger Area; instead, they resemble self-contained, locally concentrated linear commercial cores.

### C1 — Network-Embedded Commercial Corridors

C1 Corridors are well connected to the surrounding street network and, more specifically, often function as **Area-embedded commercial spines**. A large share of C1 Corridors are attached corridors inside Areas, making this the Corridor type most strongly characterized by Area embedding. They commonly serve as the main organizing axis of commercial activity within an Area, linking street segments, commercial clusters, and local nodes. The name remains appropriate, but the interpretive emphasis is on their role as the internal commercial backbone of Areas rather than on network embedding alone.

### C2 — Functionally Mixed Low-Intensity Corridors

C2 Corridors have relatively low commercial intensity but are not functionally singular. They often combine retail, food, daily services, and related uses. Most remain independent rather than serving as the internal organizing spine of a large Area. They are best understood as smaller, neighborhood-serving commercial corridors oriented to nearby residents.

### C3 — Network-Isolated Corridor Segments

C3 Corridors are relatively isolated in space and network structure, with weaker ties to other parts of the commercial system. Almost all C3 Corridors are independent rather than Area-attached, reinforcing that this type mainly represents standalone, peripheral, or spatially constrained corridor segments—not primary internal components of Areas.

### C4 — Continuous Mixed-Use Corridors

C4 Corridors represent urban commercial streets with relatively continuous commercial frontage and a mixed functional composition. Some may relate to Areas, but most remain independent corridors. Their defining traits are along-corridor commercial continuity and mixed use, rather than Area embedding itself.

---

## Small Nodes

**Small Nodes** are localized commercial concentrations that lack the spatial continuity or scale needed to form an independent commercial Area or Corridor. They typically consist of one or several closely clustered establishments that act as local activity anchors—such as small neighborhood retail clusters, corner commercial groups, or isolated service centers. Although they are not standalone commercial systems, Small Nodes help support nearby residential areas, complement larger commercial systems, and strengthen local accessibility within the broader commercial network.
